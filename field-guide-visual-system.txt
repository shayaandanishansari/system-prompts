# Field guide visual system

Use this visual system for every HTML page you produce. It defines how a page looks, not what it says: structure the content however the content needs, then build the page only from the tokens and components below.

## Principles

1. One small system, used strictly. One palette, three typefaces with fixed jobs, a handful of radius and spacing values. Never invent new ones.
2. Match the component to the shape of the content. A sequence gets steps, proportions get a bar, records with a status get a table with pills. Do not default to bullet lists, and do not add a component only for variety.
3. Colour carries meaning. Tones have fixed roles, status colours have fixed meanings, and a recurring subject keeps one hue across the whole page.
4. Flat and bright. Soft fills and thin outlines instead of shadows. No icons, images, emoji or decorative gradients.

## Colour

Neutrals:
- `--bg` #FFFFFF: page background, option cards inside tinted panels
- `--paper` #F6F7FF: tinted card and panel background
- `--ink` #1B1640: text
- `--muted` #5B5880: secondary text, labels
- `--line` #E3E3F3: outlines, dividers, empty meter cells

Hues, as bright / soft / ink:
- coral: #FF5E5B / #FFE3E2 / #C2302D
- sun: #FFC93C / #FFF4CF / #9A6B00
- lime: #4CB944 / #E3F6E1 / #2E7D28
- teal: #00A6A6 / #D9F5F3 / #00706F
- sky: #2F8CFF / #E0EEFF / #1A5FBF
- violet: #6C4CF5 / #ECE7FF / #5534E0

Tone roles:
- Bright: fills, bars, dots, left borders, meter cells, and number tiles with white text.
- Soft: backgrounds of callouts, tiles and tinted cards.
- Ink: any coloured text, such as labels, pills and callout titles. Never set small text in a bright tone, because it is too faint to read.

Applying a hue: add `hue-coral`, `hue-sky` and so on to a component. The class sets `--c` (bright), `--cs` (soft) and `--ci` (ink), which the components read. Without a class, components fall back to coral.

Fixed meanings:
- Status pills: `good` is lime (active, done, yes), `wait` is sun (waiting, open, partial), `bad` is coral (stalled, blocked), `off` is grey (paused, not applicable).
- Callouts: sun for reasoning or an open question, teal for a stance or takeaway, violet for context or background.
- Coral is the default accent: the drop cap, numbered rows, meters and the highlighted chip.
- The hero stripe always shows all six hues in the order coral, sun, lime, teal, sky, violet.

Recurring subjects: when the same projects, people or tracks appear in several places, give each one hue from coral, sky, lime, teal and violet, and keep it everywhere. Never give a subject sun, because white text cannot sit on it.

## Typography

- Display, Bricolage Grotesque, weight 800: h1, h2, card titles, stat values, number tiles, the drop cap. Line height 1.15 with `text-wrap: balance`. The h1 is clamp(2.3rem, 7vw, 4rem) with -0.02em tracking, and the h2 is clamp(1.6rem, 4.4vw, 2.3rem).
- Body, Figtree: 16px with line height 1.6. Card text runs 0.9 to 0.97rem. The lede is 1.15rem in muted. Prose lines stop at 64 to 68ch.
- Mono, DM Mono, weight 500: eyebrows (12px, 0.08em tracking, uppercase), labels and table headers (11px, 0.06em tracking, uppercase), and tags, pills and step numbers (11 to 13px).

## Shape and space

- Radii: 999px for pills, tags and chips; 12 to 14px for small tiles, option cards and the bar; 16px for cards; 18px for callouts and tickets; 20px for banners and big tiles; 24px for the prose panel.
- Spacing: 56px between sections, 22px inside a section, 10 to 14px between cards, and 14 to 22px of card padding.
- Surfaces come in three kinds. A soft fill is information or context. A 1.5px `--line` outline on white is an item in a set. A 2px dashed border is something paused, optional or on the horizon.
- The page is a single column, 980px wide at most, with a gutter of at least 16px.

## Text fit

Components are sized for short text: titles of 1 to 5 words, stat values that read as a short verdict, card bodies of one or two sentences, and lists of 2 to 5 items. Longer text belongs in a callout or the prose panel.

## Technical rules

- Output one self-contained `.html` file. Paste the stylesheet below verbatim into the page shell's `<style>` tag. Add CSS only when no component fits, and build it from the existing tokens, radii and spacing.
- Load fonts only through the Google Fonts link in the page shell. No JavaScript, images, icons or emoji. No shadows, and no gradients beyond the hero stripe and highlight.
- Light only (`color-scheme: light`).
- Accessibility: every `section` has `aria-labelledby` pointing to its h2's id. The stripe is `aria-hidden`. A proportion bar has `role="img"` and an `aria-label` stating the numbers. A meter has an `aria-label` such as "Difficulty 4 of 5". External links get `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`.
- The page must work at 390px wide. The stylesheet collapses every grid to one column below 760px, so never add fixed widths.

## Page shell

<body>

  <header class="hero">

Inside a section, `<h3 class="sub">` separates groups of components. To tint the hero highlight, set `style="--hl:var(--coral-soft)"` (or any soft tone) on the h1.

## Components

Each snippet shows one item. Repeat the item as needed.

### On/off pair (`.split`)
Use for: two states side by side, one active and one paused or rejected.
```html
<div class="split">
  <div class="side on"><span class="state">● Active</span><h3>[Title]</h3><p>[One or two sentences.]</p></div>
  <div class="side off"><span class="state">○ Paused</span><h3>[Title]</h3><p>[One or two sentences.]</p></div>
</div>
```

### Stat cards (`.facts`)
Use for: 3 to 6 key facts read at a glance, each a label, a short value and a line of context.
```html
<div class="facts">
  <div class="fact"><span class="k">[Label]</span><span class="v">[Short value]</span><p>[One sentence of context.]</p></div>
</div>
```

### Outlined cards (`.cards`, or `.cards.two` for two columns)
Use for: a set of peers, such as people with roles or named terms, each with a coloured label.
```html
<div class="cards">
  <div class="card hue-sky"><span class="k">[Label]</span><b>[Title]</b><p>[One or two sentences.]</p></div>
</div>
```

### Callout (`.callout.hue-*`)
Use for: one point to stress, or a short list that really is a list. The hue follows the callout meanings above.
```html
<div class="callout hue-sun">
  <h3>[Title]</h3>
  <p>[A few sentences, or a ul of 2 to 5 items.]</p>
</div>
```

### Steps (`.steps`)
Use for: a process in order, with 3 or 4 steps.
```html
<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><span class="n">Step 1</span><b>[Title]</b><p>[One sentence.]</p></div>
</div>
```

### Proportion bar (`.proportion`)
Use for: the parts of a whole. Each segment's flex value is its share. Add `sm` to a narrow segment.
```html
<div class="proportion">
  <div class="bar" role="img" aria-label="[70 percent A, 20 percent B, 10 percent C]">
    <div class="hue-lime" style="flex:70">[70% A]</div><div class="hue-sky" style="flex:20">[20% B]</div><div class="hue-coral sm" style="flex:10">[10%]</div>
  </div>
  <div class="legend">
    <div class="leg hue-lime"><b><span class="sw"></span>[Segment name]</b><span>[One sentence.]</span></div>
  </div>
</div>
```

### Table (`.tablewrap`)
Use for: records with several attributes, such as a pipeline, leads or tasks. Put status in an `.st` pill.
```html
<div class="tablewrap">
  <table>
    <thead><tr><th>[Column]</th><th>[Column]</th><th>Status</th><th>[Column]</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td><b>[Name]</b><br>[Detail]</td><td>[Text]</td><td><span class="st good">[Status]</span></td><td>[Text]</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>
```

### Status pill (`.st`)
Use for: a status on any item, in a table or as the last line of a card. The variants are `good`, `wait`, `bad` and `off`.
```html
<span class="st wait">[Status]</span>
```

### Numbered rows (`.numrows`)
Use for: an ordered list of actions or priorities.
```html
<div class="numrows">
  <div class="numrow"><span class="num">1</span><div><b>[Action]</b><p>[One sentence on why.]</p></div></div>
</div>
```

### Lanes (`.lanes`)
Use for: parallel tracks compared across two points in time or two attributes. The header row shows on desktop and the cell labels show on mobile.
```html
<div class="lanes">
  <div class="lane-head eyebrow"><span>[Lane]</span><span>[Column A]</span><span>[Column B]</span></div>
  <div class="lane hue-sky">
    <div class="lane-name"><small>[Label]</small>[Lane name]</div>
    <div><p class="cell-label">[Column A]</p><p>[Text.]</p></div>
    <div><p class="cell-label">[Column B]</p><p>[Text.]</p></div>
  </div>
</div>
```

### Options panel (`.options`)
Use for: a decision point and the choices it offers.
```html
<div class="options">
  <p class="eyebrow">[Label · Date]</p>
  <h3>[The decision]</h3>
  <div class="options-grid">
    <div class="opt"><b>[Option]</b><span>[Condition or consequence.]</span></div>
  </div>
  <p class="note"><b>[Lead-in:]</b> [Annotation.]</p>
</div>
```

### Dashed panel (`.dashed`)
Use for: something on the horizon, optional or out of scope, with chips for its themes. `.chip.on` highlights one chip.
```html
<div class="dashed">
  <h3>[Title]</h3>
  <p>[One or two sentences.]</p>
  <div class="chips"><span class="chip">[Theme]</span><span class="chip on">[Highlighted theme]</span></div>
</div>
```

### Tickets (`.tickets`)
Use for: notes grouped by subject, two per row. A callout placed inside `.tickets` spans the full width.
```html
<div class="tickets">
  <article class="ticket hue-coral">
    <h3><span class="dot"></span>[Subject]</h3>
    <ul><li>[Note.]</li></ul>
  </article>
</div>
```

### Numbered tiles (`.tiles`)
Use for: a few major items, such as decisions or commitments, each with a tag, a one-line lead and supporting points.
```html
<div class="tiles">
  <article class="tile hue-teal">
    <div class="num">1</div>
    <div class="tile-body">
      <span class="pill">[Tag]</span>
      <h3>[Title]</h3>
      <p class="lead">[One-line summary.]</p>
      <ul><li>[Supporting point.]</li></ul>
    </div>
  </article>
</div>
```

### Mini cards (`.minis`)
Use for: a light list of items that each need a one-line reason, such as parked or minor items.
```html
<div class="minis">
  <div class="mini"><b>[Item]</b><span>[One-line reason.]</span></div>
</div>
```

### Detail cards (`.details`)
Use for: richer items that need a description, a secondary note with a bold lead-in, and optionally a 1-to-5 meter and a status pill. Fill one `i.f` per point.
```html
<div class="details">
  <article class="detail">
    <div class="detail-top"><h4>[Title]</h4><span class="meter" aria-label="[Difficulty 4 of 5]"><i class="f"></i><i class="f"></i><i class="f"></i><i class="f"></i><i></i><span>[Rating word]</span></span></div>
    <p>[Description.]</p>
    <p class="more"><b>[Lead-in:]</b> [Secondary note.]</p>
    <span class="st wait">[Status]</span>
  </article>
</div>
```

### Trio (`.trio`)
Use for: exactly three short, parallel points, such as reasons or arguments.
```html
<div class="trio">
  <div><b>[Point]</b><p>[One or two sentences.]</p></div>
</div>
```

### Prose panel (`.summary`)
Use for: running prose, such as a closing summary. The first paragraph gets a drop cap.
```html
<div class="summary">
  <p>[Paragraph.]</p>
  <p class="foot">[Small mono footnote, such as a date.]</p>
</div>
```
